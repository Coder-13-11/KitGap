# User-testing task sheet

Use this sheet with parents, friends, club members, or someone who has run a drive or event. Run each session in the **demo** (`/demo`), so nobody needs an account and nothing real is affected. Reset the demo between participants.

**Never invent results.** Record only what participants actually did and said, and quote them only with their permission.

## Before each session

Read this to the participant:

> "I'm testing an app I built, not you. There are no wrong answers. Please think out loud as you go. I'll take notes on what happens; I won't record your name unless you say that's OK. Is it OK if I write down short quotes from what you say, without your name, in my project notes?"

Write down: date, role being tested, how the participant relates to drives (for example "ran a canned food drive at church"), device and browser, and whether they agreed to quotes.

Don't help unless they're stuck for 2 minutes or ask to stop. If you help, write down exactly what you said.

## Tasks

### A. Receiving organization (act as Maya)

1. "Your family center needs 50 hygiene kits, each with a toothbrush, toothpaste and a bar of soap, by a date about a month from now. Post that request."
   *Success:* reaches **Submit for review** with a correct supply total (50 / 50 / 50).
2. "A group has delivered kits. Find out what's waiting for you, and record that 2 of the kits were missing pencil packs."
   *(First, do steps 1–3 of the demo walkthrough as Jordan so a delivery exists.)*
   *Success:* records a partial acceptance with a reason.
3. "How many kits has the school-supply request actually received so far?"
   *Success:* gives the confirmed-received number, not the promised number.

### B. Student coordinator (act as Jordan)

1. "Your club can handle about 15 school-supply kits. Find a request and sign the club up."
   *Success:* starts a drive for 15 kits and can say what the club must collect.
2. "Two members brought 15 backpacks and 20 notebooks. Record that."
3. "What's stopping the club from packing all 15 kits right now?"
   *Success:* names the missing items (for example "10 notebooks and 15 pencil packs").
4. "Share the drive with someone who isn't in the club, without giving out any private details."
   *Success:* copies the public link, not the invitation link.

### C. Contributor (act as Sam)

1. "You can bring 6 notebooks to the club's drive. Promise that."
2. "Your plans changed. Take that promise back."
3. "Has anything you promised already arrived?"

### D. KitGap reviewer (act as Riley), optional

1. "Maya submitted a new request. Decide whether it can be listed."
   *Success:* understands they must confirm the contact first.

## After each role, ask

1. What was the hardest part?
2. Was any word or number confusing? (Point to it.)
3. What did you expect to happen that didn't?
4. Would you trust these numbers to plan a real drive? Why or why not?
5. On a scale of 1 to 5, how easy was this? (Record the number and their reason.)
